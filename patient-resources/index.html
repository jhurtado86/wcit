Your first visit

What happens at your first visit

Plan on a thorough visit. We need to understand the wound and the reasons it has not healed.

  1. 1

    We call you to schedule

    After you request an appointment, our team calls you to set up your first visit at the McAllen or Mission clinic.

  2. 2

    Check-in

    Bring the items listed on this page, and any referral paperwork your doctor gave you.

  3. 3

    The exam

    A clinician examines and measures the wound, checks the blood flow and feeling in your legs and feet, and reviews your health history and medicines.

  4. 4

    Your plan

    You leave knowing the cause we suspect and the plan to treat it: which of our seven treatments we will use, how often you will come in, and how to care for the wound at home.

  5. 5

    Follow-up visits

    Most wound care is a series of visits. Depending on the plan, that can mean weekday hyperbaric sessions, weekly dressing or cast changes, or a check every few weeks. The team reviews the wound at every visit and adjusts the plan as it changes.

What to bring

It helps to bring:

  • A photo ID and your insurance card
  • A list of your medicines and allergies
  • The names of your other doctors
  • Any referral paperwork, records, or photos of the wound
  • Your blood sugar log, if you have diabetes
  • A family member or caregiver, if you would like one with you

Before a visit

Getting ready for a visit

A few things that make every visit go more smoothly.

Dressings
Keep the dressing on and dry unless we told you it is waterproof. If it soaks through or comes off, call us before your visit.
Casts and wound VACs
Do not remove a total contact cast or a wound VAC dressing yourself. Call us if the cast feels loose, wet, or stained, or if the pump will not hold its seal.
Clothing and shoes
Wear loose clothing, and shoes that fit over a dressing or a cast shoe.
Medicines
Take your usual medicines unless we tell you otherwise, and bring the list.
Hyperbaric sessions
If you are having hyperbaric oxygen therapy, follow the chamber rules on that page: no lotions, perfume, or hair spray, and nothing electronic in the chamber.

After a visit

Caring for your wound at home

Your plan comes with instructions for home: how to care for the dressing, when to change it or leave it alone, how to keep pressure off the wound or keep a swollen leg raised, and when to come back. Following them matters as much as anything we do in the clinic.

If you are not sure about an instruction, call us. It is always better to ask than to guess.

Call us the same day if you notice

  • Redness, warmth, or swelling spreading around the wound
  • More drainage, or drainage with a bad smell
  • More pain instead of less
  • Black tissue, or a wound that looks bigger
  • Fever or chills
  • A cast that is loose, wet, or stained, or a wound VAC that will not hold its seal

In an emergency, call 911. For anything else, call (956) 317-4044 during office hours.

Insurance

Insurance and cost questions

Coverage for wound care depends on your plan and your diagnosis. Some plans require a referral from your primary care doctor, or an authorization before certain treatments, and the office will help you understand what yours requires before you start.

Request an appointment, and our team will go over your options with you before your first visit.
