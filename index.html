For patients and families

When to see a wound specialist

Most cuts and sores get better on their own. Some need more help. Time is the simplest way to tell them apart.

Weeks since the wound appeared

0 1 2 3 4 5 6

  1. The first 2 weeks

    Watch for signs that the wound is starting to heal.

  2. About 2 weeks

    Not starting to heal? The wound needs specialist care.

  3. About 6 weeks

    Not fully healed? The wound needs specialist care.

A wound also needs specialist care if you have diabetes, poor circulation or vascular disease, or other risk factors.

Conditions we treat

Chronic wounds we treat in the Rio Grande Valley

A chronic wound is one that is slow to heal or keeps coming back. Each type has its own cause, so each one needs its own plan of care.

All conditions we treat
  • Diabetic and neuropathic foot ulcers Open sores on the feet, common with diabetes and nerve damage.
  • Arterial (ischemic) ulcers Wounds caused by poor blood flow.
  • Pressure ulcers (bedsores) Skin breakdown from long periods in one position.
  • Venous stasis ulcers Leg wounds linked to vein problems and swelling.
  • Non-healing surgical wounds Incisions that reopen or stop healing after surgery.
  • Radiation wounds Skin and tissue damage after radiation treatment.
  • Burns Burn injuries that need specialized wound care.
  • Peristomal skin irritation Sore, broken skin around a stoma.
  • Fistulas Abnormal passages that drain through the skin.
  • Other chronic wounds Resistant wounds that have not responded to earlier care.

Our services

Wound care services in McAllen and Mission

Your care plan may use one treatment or several. Our team chooses them based on your wound, your health, and how you are healing.

Hyperbaric Oxygen Therapy

You rest inside a pressurized chamber and breathe 100% oxygen. The extra oxygen reaches damaged tissue and may help hard-to-heal wounds close. Sessions are usually daily.

Dr. Pedro Abanto, MD, FACP, holds the Essentials of Wound Care and Hyperbaric Medicine certification.

About hyperbaric oxygen therapy

  • Negative Pressure Wound Therapy (Wound VAC)

    Controlled suction draws fluid away from the wound and promotes new tissue growth.

  • Debridement of Wounds

    Removal of dead tissue to prepare the wound bed for healing.

  • Total Contact Casting

    A cast that spreads pressure away from a diabetic foot ulcer to promote healing.

  • Bio-engineered Skin

    Advanced skin substitutes that support the closing of chronic wounds.

  • Compression Therapy

    Compression for venous stasis ulcers and leg swelling, to improve circulation and promote healing.

  • Specialty Dressings and Ointments

    Advanced dressings and topical treatments matched to your wound type.

Our team

Certified wound specialists on our team

Hard-to-heal wounds call for a team. Ours is multidisciplinary: physicians, a wound care nurse, and nurse practitioners who plan your care together.

Two of our clinicians are certified by the American Board of Wound Management: Dr. José R. Sobrevilla, MD, CWSP, a Certified Wound Specialist Physician, and Cesar Martinez, RN, CWS, a Certified Wound Specialist. Dr. Pedro Abanto, MD, FACP, is an internal medicine physician with training in hyperbaric medicine. Our nurse practitioners bring wound care and critical care experience.

All of our care is outpatient and evidence-based, with a plan built around your wound and your overall health.
