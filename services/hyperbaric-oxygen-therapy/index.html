What it is

What is hyperbaric oxygen therapy?

Hyperbaric oxygen therapy is a treatment where you breathe 100% oxygen inside a chamber pressurized to two to three times normal. Under that pressure, your blood carries far more oxygen than it does from room air.

A wound needs oxygen to heal, and diabetes, poor circulation, swelling, and radiation damage can all cut off the supply. HBOT pushes extra oxygen into your blood plasma, which reaches tissue even where blood flow is weak. Over a course of sessions, it may help your body grow new blood vessels, fight infection, and build new tissue.

Oxygen in the air you breathe

Room air21%

Normal pressure. Oxygen travels on red blood cells, so it goes only where blood flows well.

Inside the chamber100%

Two to three times normal pressure. Oxygen also dissolves into the plasma and reaches tissue that red blood cells cannot.

More oxygen, under pressure, means more oxygen reaching the wound.

What to expect

What a session is like

Most people find HBOT simple and calm.

  1. 1

    Before your first session

    A physician examines your wound, reviews your health history and every medicine you take, and may order a simple skin oxygen test. You sign a consent form.

  2. 2

    Getting ready

    You change into a cotton gown we provide. Lighters, phones, watches, and anything with a battery stay outside. Skip lotions, perfume, and hair spray on treatment days.

  3. 3

    In the chamber

    You lie down and rest while the pressure slowly rises. You may feel pressure in your ears, like on an airplane; swallowing or yawning helps. Our team watches you the whole time.

  4. 4

    Coming back down

    Near the end, the pressure slowly returns to normal. The whole visit takes about 2 hours.

  5. 5

    After your session

    You can drive home and go about your day. You come back for your next session, usually the next weekday.

Have a question before your first session? Call us at (956) 317-4044.

Who it helps

Wounds that may respond to HBOT

HBOT is used for certain wounds that have not healed with standard care, usually after debridement, offloading, or compression have been tried.

All conditions we treat
  • Diabetic foot ulcers Deep foot wounds that have not improved after about a month of standard care.
  • Radiation wounds Tissue damage that appears months or years after radiation treatment.
  • Non-healing surgical wounds Skin grafts or flaps that are at risk of failing.
  • Chronic bone infection Osteomyelitis that keeps returning despite antibiotics.
  • Burns Some serious burns, as part of a larger care plan.
  • Arterial (ischemic) ulcers In select cases, when blood flow has been restored as far as it can be.

Is HBOT right for you?

Who it’s for

Before we recommend HBOT, a physician examines your wound, reviews what has already been tried, and may order a simple skin oxygen test.

HBOT may be a good fit if…

  • Your wound has not improved after about 4 weeks of standard care
  • You have diabetes and a deep foot ulcer
  • Your wound is in an area treated with radiation
  • A skin graft or flap is not taking
  • A bone infection has not cleared with antibiotics

We may need a closer look if…

  • You have certain lung conditions, like a collapsed lung or severe COPD
  • You have had recent ear or sinus surgery, or cannot clear your ears
  • You are taking certain chemotherapy medicines
  • You have a fever, a bad cold, or a seizure disorder
  • You are pregnant

These do not always rule it out; tell us about every health condition and medicine you take.

What about side effects? Most people feel fine. The most common issue is ear pressure. Some people notice a temporary change in vision during a course of treatment; it usually returns to normal afterward. Serious side effects are rare.

Questions about cost or insurance? Coverage depends on your plan and your diagnosis. Request an appointment and our team will help you understand your options before you start.

Questions

Hyperbaric oxygen therapy FAQ

Short answers to what patients and families ask us most.

Does hyperbaric oxygen therapy hurt?

Most people do not find HBOT painful. The most common feeling is pressure in your ears as the chamber pressurizes, like on an airplane. Swallowing or yawning helps.

How long does each session take?

Plan on about 2 hours from arrival to leaving. Most of that time is spent resting inside the chamber while the pressure rises, holds, and comes back down.

How many sessions will I need?

It depends on your wound. Many treatment plans call for 20 to 40 sessions, one each weekday. Your physician sets the number and checks your progress.

Can I drive myself home after a session?

Yes. HBOT does not use sedation or any medicine that makes you drowsy. Some people feel tired afterward, so plan a light day.

What can I bring into the chamber?

Very little. Nothing that can spark goes inside: no phones, watches, hearing aids, lighters, or batteries. You wear a cotton gown we provide.

Does insurance cover HBOT?

Some plans cover HBOT for specific wound diagnoses when certain conditions are met. Request an appointment and our team will help you understand your options.
