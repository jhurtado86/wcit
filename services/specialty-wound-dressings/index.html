What it is

What are specialty dressings?

Wounds heal best when they are kept slightly moist, protected from bacteria, and free of extra fluid. Plain gauze does none of those things well.

Each specialty dressing solves a different problem, and the right choice changes as your wound changes. So we look at the wound and choose again at every visit.

Matching the dressing to the wound

The wound is dry
A hydrogel adds moisture so new tissue can grow.
It drains heavily
Foam or alginate dressings absorb fluid and protect the skin around it.
Infection is a concern
Silver, iodine, or medical honey dressings help control bacteria.
There is dead tissue
An enzymatic ointment clears it between visits.
It is deep or stalled
Collagen gives new tissue a scaffold to grow on.
Skin around a stoma is sore
Barrier creams, powders, and rings protect it from drainage.

What to expect

What dressing care is like

Dressings are chosen at every visit, and most people change them at home in between.

  1. 1

    Looking closely

    We measure the wound and look at the tissue in it, how much it drains, how deep it is, and how the skin around it looks.

  2. 2

    Choosing the dressing

    We pick the dressing and any ointment that match your wound right now, and explain what each one does.

  3. 3

    Applying it

    We clean the wound, apply any ointment, and place the dressing. We show you or your caregiver exactly how.

  4. 4

    Caring for it at home

    You leave with written instructions, a schedule for changes, and a list of supplies. Some dressings change daily; others stay on up to a week.

  5. 5

    Checking again

    At your next visit we look at the wound and the dressing together. As the wound heals, the dressing gets simpler.

Dressing soaked through, stuck, or smelling bad, or redness spreading around the wound? Call us at (956) 317-4044.

Who it helps

Wounds and skin we treat with specialty dressings

Every chronic wound we treat gets a dressing matched to it, and so does the skin around it. Debridement clears dead tissue first, and compression or casting takes the pressure off.

All conditions we treat
  • Diabetic and neuropathic foot ulcers Dressings that manage drainage under an offloading cast or shoe.
  • Arterial (ischemic) ulcers Gentle, non-adherent dressings for fragile tissue with poor blood flow.
  • Pressure ulcers (bedsores) Absorbent and protective dressings for deep pressure injuries.
  • Venous stasis ulcers High-absorbency dressings worn under compression wraps.
  • Non-healing surgical wounds Dressings that keep an open incision moist, clean, and protected.
  • Radiation wounds Soothing, non-stick dressings for skin damaged by radiation.
  • Burns Dressings that protect healing burn surfaces and control bacteria.
  • Peristomal skin irritation Barrier creams, powders, and rings for sore skin around a stoma.
  • Fistulas Pouching systems and barrier products that manage drainage.

How we choose

Who it’s for

Everyone with a chronic wound needs the right dressing, so this is about how we choose, and how you can help the dressing do its job at home.

What decides the dressing

  • How much the wound drains, from dry to soaking
  • How deep the wound is, and whether it needs to be packed
  • Whether infection is present or likely
  • The condition of the skin around the wound
  • What you can realistically change at home, and how often

Call us before your next visit if…

  • The dressing soaks through sooner than we said it would
  • The wound smells bad, or the drainage changes color
  • Redness or swelling spreads out from the wound
  • The dressing is stuck and will not come off without pain
  • You have a fever, or you run out of supplies

Allergies matter, too. Tell us about any reaction to adhesives, silver, iodine, latex, tape, or ointments.

What about side effects? Skin irritation from adhesives is the most common issue, and a dressing that is not absorbing enough can soften the skin. Both are fixed by changing the dressing.

Questions about cost or insurance? Many dressings are covered as medical supplies when prescribed, but rules vary by plan. Request an appointment and our team will help you understand your options before you start.

Questions

Specialty dressings FAQ

Short answers to what patients and families ask us most.

Why not just use a regular bandage?

Plain gauze dries the wound out and sticks to new tissue. Specialty dressings keep the wound slightly moist, absorb the right amount of fluid, and protect against bacteria.

Can I buy these dressings at the pharmacy?

Some simple ones, yes. Most specialty dressings are medical supplies that we prescribe and order for you. Please use only what we have chosen.

How often do I change the dressing?

It depends on the dressing and the wound. Some are changed every day; others stay on for up to a week. We give you a written schedule.

Can I shower with the dressing on?

Some dressings are waterproof; most are not. We show you how to cover the wound. Never soak a wound in a bath, pool, or hot tub.

What are the signs of infection I should watch for?

Spreading redness, warmth, swelling, new or worse pain, thick or discolored drainage, a bad smell, or a fever. Call us the same day.

Does insurance cover wound dressings?

Many dressings are covered as medical supplies when prescribed, but rules vary by plan. Request an appointment and we will help you understand your options.
