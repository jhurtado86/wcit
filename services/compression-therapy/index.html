Venous leg ulcer care · Compression

Compression therapy in McAllen and Mission

Compression therapy uses wraps or stockings to gently squeeze the lower leg. That helps your veins push blood back toward the heart, brings swelling down, and gives a venous leg ulcer a chance to heal.

What it is

What is compression therapy?

The veins in your legs have one-way valves that keep blood moving up toward the heart. When they weaken, blood pools in the lower leg, the skin darkens and hardens, and eventually it breaks open near the ankle. That sore is a venous stasis ulcer.

Compression fixes the pooling from the outside, firmest at the ankle and lighter toward the knee. A multi-layer wrap while the ulcer is open; a fitted stocking after it heals.

What to expect

What compression therapy is like

Compression starts with a quick circulation check, then a wrap you wear all week.

  1. 1

    Checking circulation

    A quick test compares the blood pressure at your ankle and your arm. Compression on a leg with poor arterial flow can be harmful, so we never skip it.

  2. 2

    Cleaning and dressing the ulcer

    We clean the ulcer, remove any dead tissue, and apply a dressing that can handle the drainage.

  3. 3

    Wrapping the leg

    We wrap the leg in layers from the toes to just below the knee, firmest at the ankle. It should feel snug, not painful.

  4. 4

    Living with the wrap

    Keep the wrap on and dry. Walk every day and put your feet up when you sit. Call us right away if your toes go numb, pale, or blue.

  5. 5

    Changes, and after healing

    You come back once or twice a week for a fresh wrap and a wound check. When the ulcer closes, we fit you for stockings.

Toes numb, pale, or blue under the wrap, or pain that is getting worse? Do not loosen it yourself. Call us at (956) 317-4044.

Who it helps

Leg problems compression may help

Compression is the foundation of treatment for venous leg ulcers. An ulcer that stalls despite good compression may also be a candidate for bio-engineered skin.

All conditions we treat
  • Venous stasis ulcers Open sores on the lower leg, usually near the inside of the ankle, caused by weak vein valves.
  • Chronic leg swelling Swelling and hardened, darkened skin that signal a venous ulcer is on its way.
  • Non-healing surgical wounds on the leg Incisions that stay open because swelling keeps pulling at them.
  • Mixed arterial and venous ulcers In select cases, with lighter compression, after blood flow has been checked.

Is compression right for you?

Who it’s for

Compression helps most people with venous ulcers, but it can harm a leg with poor arterial blood flow. That is why we check circulation first, every time.

Compression may be a good fit if…

  • You have an open venous ulcer on the lower leg
  • Your legs swell, and the skin near the ankle has darkened or hardened
  • You have healed a venous ulcer before and want to keep it from returning
  • Your circulation check shows good arterial blood flow

We may need a closer look if…

  • Your arteries are narrowed (peripheral artery disease)
  • You have heart failure that is not well controlled
  • You have lost feeling in your feet from nerve damage
  • The skin on your leg is infected and needs treatment first

These do not always rule it out; tell us about every health condition and medicine you take.

What about side effects? The wrap can feel tight or itchy for a day or two, and the skin under it can get irritated. Serious problems are rare when circulation has been checked.

Questions about cost or insurance? Coverage for wraps and stockings depends on your plan and your diagnosis. Request an appointment and our team will help you understand your options before you start.

Questions

Compression therapy FAQ

Short answers to what patients and families ask us most.

Does a compression wrap hurt?

It should feel snug, not painful. Some tightness and itching in the first day or two is normal. If pain gets worse or your toes go numb, pale, or blue, call us right away.

Can I shower with the wrap on?

The wrap needs to stay dry. We show you how to cover it with a waterproof cover or a sealed bag. If it gets wet, call us.

Can I take the wrap off at night?

No. A multi-layer wrap stays on day and night until we change it. Stockings, used after healing, can usually come off at bedtime.

How long will it take for the ulcer to heal?

Weeks to months, depending on the size of the ulcer and how long it has been open. Keeping the wrap on, walking daily, and elevating your legs all help.

Will I need to wear compression stockings forever?

Most people do. Weak vein valves do not repair themselves, so daily compression is the best way to keep an ulcer from coming back.

Does insurance cover compression therapy?

Some plans cover wraps and stockings for venous ulcers when certain conditions are met. Request an appointment and we will help you understand your options.
