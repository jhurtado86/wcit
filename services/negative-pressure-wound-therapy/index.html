What it is

What is negative pressure wound therapy?

Foam is cut to fit inside the wound and sealed with a clear film. A thin tube runs to a small pump that pulls air out, so the foam gently presses against the wound.

That steady pull draws out extra fluid, brings down swelling, pulls the wound edges closer, and increases blood flow, so new tissue can fill in from the bottom up.

What to expect

What a wound VAC visit is like

The dressing is fitted in the clinic, and you go home with the pump.

  1. 1

    Fitting the dressing

    We clean the wound, remove any dead tissue, cut the foam to fit, seal it with film, and connect the pump.

  2. 2

    Turning on the pump

    The foam shrinks and firms up. You may feel pulling for a few minutes. Tell us if it hurts; we can adjust the pressure.

  3. 3

    Living with the VAC

    The pump is small and comes with a carrying case. You keep it on all the time. We show you how to charge it and bathe.

  4. 4

    Dressing changes

    You come back, usually 2 to 3 times a week, and we measure the wound. If the pump alarms or the seal leaks, call us the same day.

  5. 5

    Finishing

    When the wound is shallow and filled with healthy tissue, we switch to a simpler dressing or another treatment.

Pump alarming, or the seal came loose? Do not try to fix the dressing yourself. Call us at (956) 317-4044.

Who it helps

Wounds a wound VAC may help

A wound VAC works best on a clean, deep wound. Dead tissue is cleared with debridement first, and a foot ulcer may also need total contact casting.

All conditions we treat
  • Diabetic foot ulcers Deep foot wounds, including those left after an amputation.
  • Pressure ulcers (bedsores) Deep pressure injuries over the hips, tailbone, or heels.
  • Non-healing surgical wounds Incisions that have opened up, and grafts or flaps that need support.
  • Venous stasis ulcers Large or deep leg ulcers, in select cases, alongside compression.
  • Burns Some deep burns, and burn areas that have received a skin graft.

Is a wound VAC right for you?

Who it’s for

Before we start, a physician checks the wound for infection and dead tissue and makes sure the blood flow can support healing.

A wound VAC may be a good fit if…

  • Your wound is deep and needs to fill in with new tissue
  • Your wound drains a lot of fluid
  • An incision has opened up after surgery
  • A skin graft or flap needs to be held in place

We may need a closer look if…

  • There is dead tissue or untreated infection in the wound
  • The wound sits over an exposed blood vessel, nerve, or organ
  • Cancer is present in the wound
  • You take blood thinners, or blood flow to the area is poor

These do not always rule it out; tell us about every health condition and medicine you take.

What about side effects? The film can irritate the skin, and dressing changes can be uncomfortable for a few minutes. Bleeding is uncommon.

Questions about cost or insurance? Coverage for the pump and supplies depends on your plan and your wound. Request an appointment and our team will help you understand your options before you start.

Questions

Wound VAC FAQ

Short answers to what patients and families ask us most.

Does a wound VAC hurt?

Most people feel pulling when the pump first turns on; it fades within minutes. Tell us if anything hurts.

Can I shower with a wound VAC?

Often, yes. The pump can be disconnected briefly for bathing as long as the dressing stays sealed. We show you how.

How long will I need the VAC?

It depends on how deep the wound is and how fast it fills in. Some wounds need it for a few weeks, others longer.

What do I do if the pump alarms?

Most alarms mean a small leak or a full canister. Check that the tubing is not kinked. If it does not stop, call us the same day.

Can I sleep with the pump on?

Yes. Keep the pump on and the tubing free of kinks. Most people set the pump on the nightstand.

Does insurance cover a wound VAC?

Some plans cover the pump and supplies for specific wound types when certain conditions are met. Request an appointment and we will help you understand your options.
