Total contact casting for diabetic foot ulcers

What it is

Why a diabetic foot ulcer needs a cast

Diabetes can damage the nerves in your feet, so you may not feel a sore forming under the ball of the foot. Because it does not hurt, you keep walking on it, and every step presses on the same spot. That is how a small sore becomes a diabetic foot ulcer, and why these ulcers lead to so many infections and amputations.

Taking the pressure off, called offloading, is the single most important part of healing it, and a total contact cast is considered the most effective way to do that. Molded closely to your foot and lower leg, from the toes to just below the knee, it spreads your weight across all of it so the ulcer carries almost none.

And you cannot take it off. A removable boot protects the ulcer only while it is worn. The cast protects it with every single step.

What to expect

What total contact casting is like

The cast goes on in one clinic visit and is changed every week or two until the ulcer closes.

  1. 1

    Checking the foot

    A physician examines the ulcer and checks the blood flow in your foot. Infection or dead tissue is treated first.

  2. 2

    Applying the cast

    We dress the ulcer, pad the bony spots, and mold the cast closely to your foot and lower leg. A cast shoe goes over it.

  3. 3

    Walking in the cast

    You can walk, but less is better. Keep trips short, keep the cast dry, and if it is on your right foot, do not drive.

  4. 4

    Cast changes

    You come back every 1 to 2 weeks. We remove the cast, measure the ulcer, check the skin, and apply a new one. Call right away if the cast feels loose, gets wet, or shows drainage.

  5. 5

    After the ulcer closes

    New skin is fragile. We fit you with protective footwear, teach you to check your feet every day, and keep following up.

Cast loose, wet, or stained, or a new fever? Do not wait for your next visit. Call us at (956) 317-4044.

Who it helps

Foot wounds a total contact cast may help

Total contact casting does one job: taking pressure off a wound on the bottom of the foot. An ulcer that still will not close in the cast may be a candidate for hyperbaric oxygen or bio-engineered skin.

All conditions we treat
  • Diabetic foot ulcers Ulcers on the ball of the foot, under the toes, or on the midfoot.
  • Neuropathic foot ulcers Pressure ulcers on the sole from nerve damage with causes other than diabetes.
  • Non-healing surgical wounds on the foot Wounds left after a toe or partial foot amputation that keep reopening under pressure.
  • Charcot foot A diabetic condition in which the foot bones weaken and the arch collapses.

Is a total contact cast right for you?

Who it’s for

Before we apply a cast, a physician checks your circulation, looks for infection, and makes sure you can walk safely in a cast and keep it dry.

A total contact cast may be a good fit if…

  • You have a diabetic foot ulcer on the bottom of the foot
  • The ulcer is clean, with no deep infection
  • Blood flow to your foot is good enough to heal
  • You can walk steadily and will keep trips short
  • You can keep the cast dry and come back for changes

We may need a closer look if…

  • Blood flow to your foot is poor
  • The ulcer, or the bone beneath it, is infected
  • The ulcer is very deep or drains heavily
  • You are unsteady on your feet or have had falls
  • Your legs swell a lot from day to day

These do not always rule it out; tell us about every health condition and medicine you take.

What about side effects? The cast can rub and cause a new sore, which is why we check the skin at every change. Your calf may weaken a little. High blood sugar slows healing no matter how good the cast is, so we work with you to keep it in range.

Questions about cost or insurance? Coverage for casting depends on your plan and your diagnosis. Request an appointment and our team will help you understand your options before you start.

Questions

Total contact casting FAQ

Short answers to what patients and families ask us most.

Can I walk in a total contact cast?

Yes, with the cast shoe we fit over it. But the less you walk, the faster the ulcer heals. Keep trips short.

Can I drive with the cast on?

If the cast is on your right foot, no. If it is on your left foot and you drive an automatic, ask us before you drive.

How do I shower with a total contact cast?

The cast must stay dry. We show you how to cover it with a waterproof cast cover, and we recommend a shower chair. A wet cast needs to be changed.

How long until the ulcer heals?

It depends on the ulcer and on your blood flow and blood sugar. Many ulcers close in 6 to 12 weeks of casting.

Why not just wear a boot?

A boot works only while it is on, and most people take it off more than they think. A cast cannot be removed, so the ulcer is protected with every step.

What if the cast feels loose, or I see a stain on it?

Call us the same day. A loose cast can rub and cause a new sore. A stain, a bad smell, or a fever can mean infection.

Does insurance cover total contact casting?

Some plans cover casting for diabetic foot ulcers when certain conditions are met. Request an appointment and we will help you understand your options.
