Our services

Seven ways we treat a wound that won’t heal

Each treatment solves a different problem. Your plan may use one of them or several, and it changes as your wound heals.

  • Flagship therapy

    Hyperbaric Oxygen Therapy

    You rest inside a pressurized chamber and breathe 100% oxygen. The extra oxygen reaches tissue that blood flow alone cannot, and that may help a stalled wound start to heal.

    Often used for: diabetic foot ulcers that have not healed with standard care, radiation wounds, failing skin grafts, and chronic bone infections.

    About hyperbaric oxygen therapy

  • Deep or draining wounds

    Negative Pressure Wound Therapy (Wound VAC)

    A sealed foam dressing and a small portable pump draw fluid out of the wound and help new tissue fill it in.

    Often used for: deep diabetic foot ulcers, pressure ulcers, and incisions that have opened up.

    About wound VAC therapy

  • Usually the first step

    Debridement of Wounds

    Dead, damaged, or infected tissue is removed, most often right in the clinic, so healthy tissue can grow.

    Often used for: almost every chronic wound at some point.

    About debridement

  • Diabetic foot ulcers

    Total Contact Casting

    A closely molded cast spreads your weight across the whole lower leg so the ulcer under your foot carries almost none of it.

    Often used for: diabetic and neuropathic foot ulcers on the bottom of the foot.

    About total contact casting

  • Stalled wounds

    Bio-engineered Skin

    A thin lab-made sheet of cells, collagen, or donated tissue gives your body a scaffold and growth signals to close the wound.

    Often used for: clean diabetic foot and venous ulcers that have stopped closing.

    About bio-engineered skin

  • Venous leg ulcers

    Compression Therapy

    Graduated wraps and stockings push pooled blood back up the leg, bring swelling down, and let a venous ulcer heal.

    Often used for: venous stasis ulcers and chronic leg swelling.

    About compression therapy

  • Every wound, between visits

    Specialty Dressings and Ointments

    Foams, alginates, hydrogels, collagen, antimicrobial dressings, and barrier products, matched to your wound and changed as it heals.

    Often used for: every wound we treat, and the skin around stomas and fistulas.

    About specialty dressings

How we choose

How we build your plan

Every plan starts the same way: a physician examines the wound, measures it, and looks for the reason it has not healed. Poor circulation, pressure, swelling, infection, and high blood sugar are the usual causes, and each one points to a different treatment.

From there, the plan is built in layers. Dead tissue comes off first. Pressure or swelling is taken off next, with a cast or compression. The right dressing goes on. If the wound still stalls, we add the advanced therapies: negative pressure, a skin substitute, or hyperbaric oxygen.

Two of our clinicians are certified by the American Board of Wound Management: Dr. José R. Sobrevilla, MD, CWSP, and Cesar Martinez, RN, CWS. They work alongside our physicians and nurse practitioners, who bring wound care and critical care experience, and the whole team reviews your wound at every visit.
