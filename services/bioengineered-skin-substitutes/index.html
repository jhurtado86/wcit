Bio-engineered skin for wounds that won’t close

What it is

What is bio-engineered skin?

A chronic wound often stalls because the tissue in it has stopped sending the signals that tell the body to build new skin. A skin substitute is a thin sheet, made in a lab, placed over the wound to restart that process.

It gives new cells a scaffold to grow on and releases growth factors that encourage your own tissue to fill in. It is not a first step: it works on a clean wound that has not responded to standard care.

Good to know

How it differs from a skin graft

  • Nothing is taken from another part of your body, so there is no second wound.
  • It is placed in the clinic, not an operating room, and takes minutes.
  • Your body absorbs it and builds its own new tissue in its place.
  • Products come from donated human tissue, animal tissue, or lab-grown cells. Tell us about allergies or preferences.

What to expect

What a skin substitute visit is like

The sheet is applied in minutes. Most of the work is preparing the wound and protecting the sheet afterward.

  1. 1

    Preparing the wound

    Dead tissue is removed, infection is treated, and pressure or swelling is brought under control. A sheet placed on an unprepared wound does not take.

  2. 2

    Applying the sheet

    We clean the wound, trim the sheet to size, lay it on the wound bed, and secure it with a dressing. It takes a few minutes.

  3. 3

    Protecting it

    The dressing stays on until your next visit. Keep the area dry, keep pressure off the wound, and keep wearing your compression or cast shoe.

  4. 4

    Weekly checks

    You come back, often once a week, so we can measure the wound. If it still needs support, we apply another sheet.

  5. 5

    Closing and after

    When the wound closes, the new skin is fragile. We keep protecting it with a light dressing and the right footwear or compression.

Dressing came off, or the wound looks red, swollen, or wet? Call us before your next visit. Call us at (956) 317-4044.

Who it helps

Wounds bio-engineered skin may help

Skin substitutes are used on clean, well-supplied chronic wounds that have stopped closing. Most are treated first with debridement and either total contact casting or compression.

All conditions we treat
  • Diabetic foot ulcers Clean foot ulcers that have not closed after about a month of good care and offloading.
  • Venous stasis ulcers Leg ulcers that have stalled despite compression therapy.
  • Pressure ulcers (bedsores) Clean pressure injuries, in select cases, once pressure has been relieved.
  • Non-healing surgical wounds Incisions or open surgical sites that have stopped closing.
  • Burns Some partial-thickness burns that are slow to heal.

Is a skin substitute right for you?

Who it’s for

A skin substitute only helps when the wound is ready. A physician checks for infection and dead tissue, confirms blood flow, and reviews what has been tried.

A skin substitute may be a good fit if…

  • Your wound is clean and free of infection
  • Blood flow to the wound is good enough to heal
  • The wound has not improved after about 4 weeks of good wound care
  • Pressure or swelling on the wound is under control

We may need a closer look if…

  • There is infection or dead tissue in the wound
  • Blood flow to the area is poor
  • Cancer is present in the wound
  • You are allergic to certain animal products, or have preferences about donated tissue

These do not always rule it out; tell us about every health condition and medicine you take.

What about side effects? They are uncommon. The dressing can irritate the skin, and a sheet sometimes does not take and has to be reapplied.

Questions about cost or insurance? Coverage rules for skin substitutes vary by plan and by wound; we check yours before we start. Request an appointment and our team will help you understand your options before you start.

Questions

Bio-engineered skin FAQ

Short answers to what patients and families ask us most.

Is a skin substitute real skin from a donor?

Some products come from donated human tissue, such as amniotic membrane; others from animal collagen or lab-grown cells. All are screened under strict safety rules.

Does applying a skin substitute hurt?

Most people feel little. The sheet is laid on the cleaned wound bed and covered. Any discomfort usually comes from the cleaning, not the sheet.

How many applications will I need?

It depends on your wound. Some close after a few applications; others need more. Your physician decides at each visit.

Can I shower after a skin substitute is applied?

Keep the dressing dry until your next visit unless we tell you otherwise. We show you how to cover the area. Never soak the wound.

What if the sheet does not take?

Usually pressure, swelling, moisture, or infection is the reason. We fix the cause and apply another sheet when the wound is ready.

Does insurance cover skin substitutes?

Coverage rules vary by plan and by the type of wound. We check your coverage before we start. Request an appointment and we will help you understand your options.
